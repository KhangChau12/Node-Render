These frames are rendered by the kit, straight from scene code an AI can write. Filter by subject; the chips name the components each scene uses.

Every frame is rendered from the kit's examples, in the language you are reading. Lectures can be written in English or Vietnamese; numbers and map labels follow the lecture's language.

Built so that the video is right, not just pretty

Generated animation usually fails in the same few ways: overlapping text, visuals that ignore the voice, frames that never move. Each of these is handled by the system, not left to luck.

01

Synced to the narration, word by word

Scenes refer to words of the narration, not to seconds. When the timing changes, or real voiceover replaces the estimate, every visual stays on its word.

Take two points on the curve. The line through them is a secant2.1s. Now drag the second point5.4s closer, until the secant becomes the tangent8.9s.
02

Checks its own work

Every version is compiled and linted, then screenshotted and shown to a vision model that looks for overlaps, overflow and unreadable text and repairs them.

Edit any frame by talking

Pause the preview, attach the frame or a marked-up screenshot, and describe the change. Every version is kept, one click to undo.

The Lecture Kit

The building blocks the AI writes scenes with. Each one is a Remotion component with a Zod schema: props are validated at runtime, errors go back to the AI to fix, and the same schema generates the docs it reads. Click any component for its props and example.
